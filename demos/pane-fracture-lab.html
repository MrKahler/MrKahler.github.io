Annealed

Ordinary float glass with little internal stress. Radial cracks run out from the hit and concentric cracks circle it. Fast cracks fork, cracks bend to meet edges and other cracks at close to right angles, and a crack stops when the stress behind it runs out or it reaches an existing break.

Impactor size

A small, hard impactor on glass thicker than about its own radius punches a cone-shaped hole with a frosted rim and few cracks. A large or blunt one bends the pane instead, which produces the radial web and a bigger knocked-out hole.

Thickness and tempering

Bending stiffness rises with the cube of thickness, so thicker panes need much more energy for the same damage. Tempered glass has surface compression of at least 10,000 psi (about 69 MPa) under ASTM C1048, so it shrugs off blunt hits, but once broken it dices into small cubes all at once.

Damage thresholds are tuned to look plausible, not derived from fracture mechanics. One screen pixel is treated as about one millimeter.
