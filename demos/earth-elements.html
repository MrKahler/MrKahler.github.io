Where do these numbers come from?

All percentages are by weight (mass) unless you switch to "By atoms," which counts atoms instead. Light elements like hydrogen look tiny by weight but huge by atom count.

  • Crust, oceans, air: measured directly from huge numbers of rock, water, and air samples. These are the most reliable numbers here.
  • Mantle: estimated from mantle rocks brought up by volcanoes plus lab experiments. Good, but not exact.
  • Core: nobody has ever sampled it. Scientists infer it from how earthquake waves travel, Earth's overall density, and metal-rich meteorites. Iron and nickel are certain; the light elements (silicon, sulfur, oxygen) are educated estimates.
  • Living things: rough estimates. The split between plants, microbes, and animals uses carbon-weight estimates (Bar-On, Phillips & Milo, PNAS 2018); total wet weight is approximate.

Main references: McDonough (2003) core model; McDonough & Sun (1995) mantle; Rudnick & Gao (2003) continental crust; typical mid-ocean-ridge basalt for ocean-floor crust; standard seawater (3.5% salt); standard dry air.
